<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Final Statement</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">&#8216;My entire life is proof of my love for people. And for my country. Everything I have done has been aimed at making the world a better place, not destroying it.&#8217;</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Your Honour, to me, extremism means aggression, intolerance, an inability to find constructive solutions, obsession, selfishness, irresponsibility, and immaturity. The prosecution alleges that I support an extremist ideology and knowingly donated 3,500 roubles to an extremist organisation. This is not true.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I cannot betray myself and admit to something I did not do, even in the hope of receiving a lighter sentence. I do not share extremist views, and here is why. Forty years ago, I graduated from the Faculty of Philosophy and Economics in the Soviet Union. It was there that political and ideological personnel were trained.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And I was a good student. Since then, I have also learned that, firstly, any radical upheavals, uprisings, or revolutions that occur in countries and result in the collapse of state institutions plunge those countries into years of chaos, poverty, civil wars, and gang terror. This has been repeatedly confirmed across every continent on our planet. Syria, Venezuela, Libya, Afghanistan–there are quite a few such countries.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Secondly, any attempts to fight the state through violence, even with the best of intentions, only lead to the state becoming more repressive, freedoms being curtailed, and civil rights being restricted. This rule works like clockwork, too.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I love my country, and I want none of this for it, or, consequently, for myself. I live here, and after the collapse of the Soviet Union, I consciously chose Russian citizenship.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yes, I am not satisfied with everything. I believe you would agree that many things could be organised better. But I know that nine women cannot give birth to a child in one month. That is why I support the evolutionary development of the state through education and the humanisation of society. This is precisely why I belong to the Yabloko party, whose ideas are closest to my own, rather than to any other political party or public association. To me, wanting to change one&#8217;s country through violence and extremist methods is like demolishing one&#8217;s own home simply because not everything about it is satisfactory, instead of taking care of it and improving it as circumstances allow.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I enjoy making the world around me a better place without destroying anything. Throughout my entire adult life, I have been engaged in socially beneficial activities and helping people. My business helped others establish their own enterprises and make them more efficient. We were involved in creating things in the most literal sense: we designed hotels, residential buildings, outpatient clinics, sports facilities, restaurants, and industrial enterprises. I was president of an association of consulting companies whose purpose was to bring together and develop the professional community of business consultants across the CIS, upholding high professional standards.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">At the same time, I served as chair of my apartment building&#8217;s residents&#8217; council. I oversaw a major renovation, and for three years I worked to find constructive solutions to various problems. As a result, the renovation was completed to a high standard, on schedule, and without a single change of contractor–something that is quite rare in itself.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I am registered on the Active Citizen platform and take part in the development of Moscow through it because I firmly believe that people should participate rather than stand in the way.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For almost the last ten years, I worked on election commissions. I conscientiously fulfilled my duties, helping to ensure that citizens could exercise their electoral rights and that the electoral system functioned effectively.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">At the same time, I never neglected my original education in psychology. I continually pursued further professional development, was a member of the Psychotherapeutic League, and, in my capacity as a psychologist, helped victims of domestic violence, people who had attempted suicide, and those recovering from substance dependencies.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I ran a large online chat group for people going through difficult life circumstances. Many of them were wives of participants in the Special Military Operation. In this group, people received emotional support, psychological assistance, and consultations. Since my departure, there have been no professional psychologists left in the group, and it is now impossible to know how long it will continue to operate.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">My husband and I spent the last six months writing a book for people leaving abusive relationships, commissioned by AST Publishing House. It may now remain unfinished on the computer that was seized from me.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">My entire life is proof of my love for people. And for my country. Everything I have done has been aimed at making the world a better place, not destroying it. I have always been drawn to the work of non-profit charitable organisations carrying out socially significant projects that may not attract as much attention as fundraising campaigns for children&#8217;s medical treatment, but are no less in need of financial support. These include animal protection, environmental initiatives such as monitoring air quality in Moscow, the resocialisation of homeless people–many of whom grew up in orphanages–and human rights advocacy. In 2022 and 2023, I also actively helped refugees from the newly incorporated Russian territories. I provided both money and essential items until state assistance became more organised. My workload did not allow me to become deeply involved in this work as well, so I fulfilled my desire to help through donations.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I would usually make one-off donations of up to 1,000 roubles. The exception was the Nochlezhka NGO, to whose monthly donation programme I had consciously subscribed for many years. I donated both to individuals and to organisations. Although my payments to individual projects were relatively small, the total amount often reached several thousand roubles a month.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It is hardly surprising that, amid such a stream of transactions, I could have made a mistake and failed to notice the deductions. During questioning, I said that I must have been under considerable emotional distress at the time of the payment to the Anti-Corruption Foundation (FBK), because I could not have made such a payment in my right mind. An aggressive, extremist approach to anything is alien to me. It contradicts my beliefs, values, and principles in life. I prefer to negotiate, explain, and seek compromises and solutions that work best for everyone. That is precisely why, although I do not remember making this payment or what motivated me at the time, I am certain that I could not have knowingly transferred money to support extremist activities.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I deeply regret making this transfer, acknowledge my responsibility, and hope that the court will reach a fair and humane decision proportionate to my culpability and the amount transferred. I ask you to take into account the fact that, both in the five years preceding this payment and in the five years since, I have never done anything that would indicate support for an extremist ideology.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Your Honour, I would also like to say a few words about my family. I ask you to show mercy and compassion towards my 85-year-old mother, who came to live with me from Vladivostok, intending to spend her final days here. I took her into my home just two months before my detention.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And towards my husband, who, in his old age, has been left alone to care for my seriously ill mother, whom he had seen no more than ten times in the thirty years of our marriage.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That is all. Thank you.</p>
