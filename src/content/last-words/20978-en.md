<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Final Statement</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">&#8216;And in 2020, the year of the presidential election and the events that followed, we adhered to the fundamental principle of journalism–portraying life as it is, without imposing our own judgments on it.&#8217;</h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In September, Belarus widely celebrated National Unity Day, commemorating the unification of the Belarusian people into one family in September 1939. On 29 October 1939, the People&#8217;s Assembly of Western Belarus adopted a declaration in Białystok on the incorporation of Western Belarus into the Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic–that is, Belarus.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This was preceded by two main speeches. One of them, on the nature of government in Western Belarus, was delivered by Siarhei Asipovich Prytytski, after whom a street in Maladzyechna is named. Today, the Investigative Committee of the Republic of Belarus is located on that street.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The second speech, on the incorporation of Western Belarus into the BSSR, was delivered by Flor Danatovich Mantsevich, a teacher from the Disna region (in what is now the Vitebsk Region). Flor Danatovich Mantsevich was a teacher and folklorist, and the first teacher of the renowned Hennadz Ivanavich Tsitovich, People&#8217;s Artist of the USSR and founder and director of the Belarusian Academic National Folk Choir. He was a correspondent for the first Belarusian-language newspaper, &#8216;Nasha Niva&#8217;, and in 1939 delivered this speech at the People&#8217;s Assembly. In 1941, he was executed by the Nazi occupiers in Polotsk precisely because of his patriotic activities.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I am not passing judgment on the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, as is often done in connection with the events of 1939. I am merely pointing out that today, in this courtroom, the grandson of the Belarusian teacher who proclaimed to the whole world in 1939 the aspirations of millions of Belarusians, and not only Belarusians, to live together as one family, is being tried for discrediting Belarus.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">First and foremost, I would like to thank my family, my relatives and loved ones, my friends and colleagues, who have stood by me throughout this difficult time, supported me physically and emotionally, and helped sustain my confidence in the righteousness of my cause.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8216;Regіянальная газета&#8217; (Regional Newspaper) is the only mass-circulation local non-state publication in Belarus that is regularly published entirely in the Belarusian language. Its first issue appeared in 1995, and it remained so until recently, faithfully fulfilling its mission.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And in 2020, the year of the presidential election and the events that followed, we adhered to the fundamental principle of journalism–portraying life as it is, without imposing our own judgments on it.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Incidentally, our mission statement, a document adopted by the editorial team, explicitly states that the newspaper cannot serve as the mouthpiece for the position or interests of any political party, social movement, or religious denomination. We have always adhered to these principles and rules.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That is why it was impossible to find any calls for extremism or other destructive actions in our publications, despite such allegations being made against me and our newspaper. Perhaps this is why Prosecutor Shukrinov writes the following in his report on the examination of the YouTube channel &#8216;Naviny Maladzechna. Regіянальная газета&#8217;: &#8216;The authors have covertly posted calls to participate in unauthorised mass events, encouraging both their organisation and participation in them.&#8217; Or: &#8216;&#8230;an examination of the information disseminated has established that the resource in question spreads the opinion that repressions are being carried out against certain groups of people in the Republic of Belarus and promotes the idea of the use of repressive methods.&#8217;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yet not a single specific fact has been cited, either then or now. Article 369-1 of the Criminal Code concerns the dissemination of &#8216;knowingly false information&#8217;. The editorial policy of &#8216;Regіянальная газета&#8217;, however, was based on presenting facts, not interpretations. We did not disseminate knowingly false information. The right to freedom of opinion is guaranteed by Article 33 of the Constitution of the Republic of Belarus and Article 19 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, which Belarus has ratified.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There have been eight court proceedings concerning the designation of publications or the resources of &#8216;Regіянальная газета&#8217; as extremist materials. Unfortunately, I am not familiar with the documents from the most recent one. My defence counsel and I requested that these documents be added to the case file, but our request was denied. However, I would like to emphasise that seven other rulings, issued by different courts and different judges, contain exactly the same wording: &#8216;The court sees no grounds for commissioning expert examinations to assess the materials, since these materials are manifestly extremist in nature and no specialised knowledge is required to assess them.&#8217; Well, this is nothing short of sophistry.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Meanwhile, the case file contains documents confirming that we made no calls of any kind. These are police documents, but I would go further: they were prepared on the basis of expert opinions:</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8216;The submitted transcripts of the audio recordings of the videos &#171;Protests in Maladzyechna. Part 1. How It All Began. 9 August&#187; and &#171;Protests in Maladzyechna. Part 2. The Second Day. 10 August&#187; contain no exhortative statements calling for violent actions by one group of people against another group of people identified by their affiliation with law enforcement agencies or public officials of the Republic of Belarus. The submitted transcripts of the audio recordings from the videos contain no statements expressing a negative assessment of an individual or group of individuals identified by their affiliation with law enforcement agencies or public officials of the Republic of Belarus.&#8217;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If the public prosecutor knows that such documents exist in the case file, why does he not request expert examinations of the materials listed in the indictment? Or at least agree to have the documents from the cases reported on by the newspaper added to the case file? It would then become absolutely clear that my actions do not, in fact, constitute a criminal offence.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">On 17 March 2023, following a series of court proceedings, I was first charged with an offence under Article 369-1. From one indictment to the next–there were four in total–the same allegations were carried over: that I &#8216;am an adherent of a political movement and support a delinquent position&#8230;&#8217;, that I &#8216;have demonstrated an immoral interest in extremist activities&#8230;&#8217;, and that the crime was committed under aggravating circumstances, motivated by political and ideological enmity. Where did all this come from? I said this during the investigation and under questioning: I have never publicly or privately expressed any political or social affiliations, nor have I ever disclosed which church, if any, I attend. I considered such disclosures not merely unnecessary, but harmful for a journalist and newspaper publisher. That is why it is simply impossible to establish that I &#8216;was an adherent of some political movement&#8217;.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Articles 16 and 356 of the Code of Criminal Procedure state that &#8216;a verdict may not be based on assumptions&#8217;. I believe that the designation of a number of &#8216;Regіянальная газета&#8217; resources and publications as extremist materials, on which my criminal case is based, without proper expert examination, is precisely such an assumption, an act of presumption. That is the first point.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Second, the assertion that I &#8216;demonstrated an immoral interest in extremist activities&#8217; and was a supporter of them is equally an assumption, an act of presumption. Third, the claim that I was motivated by political and ideological enmity is also an assumption. And this is where the presumption of innocence comes into play. The elements of a criminal offence are absent.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As for the allegation that I &#8216;acted as part of a group of individuals&#8217;–of course I did. I was the head of an editorial office, and naturally I worked with other people. An editorial office is made up of people. It is thanks to this team that &#8216;Regіянальная газета&#8217; became a well-known, recognisable, authoritative and respected publication over its 28 years of existence.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There is plenty of evidence and numerous examples of this. But just go outside today and conduct a survey among ordinary people, among passers-by, and you will find confirmation of what I am saying. So, who are these people from &#8216;RG&#8217;?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Nina Mantsevich, deputy director and chief accountant, my support in everything and my life partner. She has done a great deal for &#8216;Regіянальная газета&#8217;, and without her, the newspaper would never have existed. She knows that I have never violated anything, anywhere, at any time–unless you count the time I led her, despite her sore legs, across the road outside a pedestrian crossing, for which I received an administrative warning.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Zoya Khrutskaya, deputy editor-in-chief, the author of numerous ideas, projects and articles, and the head of our creative journalistic team. She has collected people&#8217;s stories and life histories, has never violated journalistic standards, and has taught others to uphold them.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Maria Bronislavovna Beresneva, literary editor and one of the longest-serving members of the editorial office. She taught younger colleagues how to write properly. Working alongside her, young employees gained valuable experience and learned to understand the meaning and weight of every word. She was also someone who safeguarded the integrity of what we said and what we wrote about, and who would never have allowed any extremism or calls for destructive action to appear in our publications.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Oksana Yaroshonok, correspondent, writer, poet and translator, knows how to write about culture, history and human lives, and loves doing so. Together with a reader from the Smorgon region named Zhilko, she uncovered and brought back from oblivion dozens of names of Belarusians–sons of Belarus who died on Polish territory in 1939, among the first in Europe to bear the brunt of the Nazi invasion. They were buried in the town of Piotrków Trybunalski. Their families did not know what had happened to their loved ones. I personally know one family, the Steburako family, in which one brother found the grave of his brother.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A year later, the brother in Belarus who had lived long enough to receive this news passed away. But how much more peacefully he must have departed this life, knowing what had become of his brother, whom he had seen off to war! And Oksana remained alongside the reader until her very last day.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Igor Polynsky, who has been mentioned here, is a technical editor, poet, musician, journalist and, if I may put it this way, a multi-instrumentalist. He is so versatile that he can do almost anything in journalism and its technical production. That is why his official position was technical editor, although he also wrote, filmed, spoke and produced excellent journalistic material.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Anastasia Utkina is the youngest member of our team. She loves sports and writing about sports, and she loves photography. It was the photo reports by Anastasia Utkina and Zoya Khrutskaya, as well as Zoya Khrutskaya&#8217;s and Igor Polynsky&#8217;s videos and articles, that documented what happened in 2020, while adhering to high professional standards.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Zhanna Gorban, our advertising specialist, is an honest and meticulous person, in the best sense of the word. Thoroughness and attention to detail are essential when collecting advertising materials and payments. She was responsible for advertising and finances, and we could always be absolutely confident that the company&#8217;s cash register was in safe hands with Zhanna.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Unfortunately, today the cash register is empty, all of &#8216;RG&#8217;s&#8217; resources have been designated as extremist, its employees have lost their jobs, and its editor-in-chief stands accused of discrediting the Republic of Belarus. I am proud to have worked alongside these people, a team of true professionals for whom the truth mattered more than convenience. They could sleep peacefully after every publication because they had written only the truth. I could sleep just as peacefully because I never had to worry that we were supposedly publishing lies or slandering anyone. And, of course, our team was never involved in extremism.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Throughout my journalistic career, I have had the privilege of meeting many different people. I knew Hennadz Aleksandrovich Kakhanovsky, Adam Asipovich Maldzis, Anatoly Valentinovich Rogach, Vladimir Sodal, Yanka Solomevich, Nikolai Kopylovich, Mikhail Mikhailovich Chernyavsky, and Konstantin Ivanovich Khoroshevich. They certainly would not have admitted into their circle of acquaintances–or, in some cases, I would even say friends–people who were indifferent to Belarus or careless in their attitude towards it.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Even today, I can hear their voices–the voices of those who, together with my ancestors, gave me clear principles to guide me through life. They taught me to live by the principle: &#8216;Never betray your homeland.&#8217; And to me, my homeland is not merely the spring in the village of Malaya Kovalevshchina in the Miory region, which is still known as Flor&#8217;s Spring because my grandfather, the teacher Flor Danatovich Mantsevich, cleared it together with his pupils, and its waters still flow today. Nor is it merely the well in Ikazn in the Braslaw region, known as Mantsevich&#8217;s Well because my grandfather began building a house there–but 1941 prevented him from finishing it, while the well remained. My homeland is also my people, my Belarus, to whose service I have devoted my life.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A year ago, my father, journalist and writer Boris Florovich Mantsevich, passed away. I was unable to erect a monument for him on the anniversary of his death because that cannot be done from prison. But for writers, their books become their monuments. His last book published during his lifetime, released in 2021 and dedicated to his fellow student at the Faculty of Philology, with whom he studied and was friends, Nil Semyonovich Hilevich, bears the symbolic title &#8216;Belarushchyna&#8217;. Today, the son of the author of this book is on trial for discrediting the Republic of Belarus, despite the prosecution having failed to produce a single fact to support the charge–as my defence counsel and I have demonstrated–and despite every allegation being refutable.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I am not a dreamer, but I believe that one day, in Maladzyechna, or Vileyka, Myadzel or Svir, or perhaps in the small village of Verkhniya Druyi in the Maladzyechna region, a street named after &#8216;Regіянальная газета&#8217; may well become a reality. Despite everything that has happened to &#8216;Regіянальная газета&#8217;, it has written a bright and worthy line in the history of modern Belarus. The newspaper genuinely strengthened the standing of the Belarusian nation, gave people truthful reporting in a language close to their hearts, and always shared the concerns and problems of its readers and their neighbours.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In textbooks on the history of Belarusian journalism, those who study it–and there will always be such people, because journalism is one of the oldest professions–will certainly find accounts of the finest work produced by the &#8216;Regіянальная газета&#8217; team, which called upon its journalists, and was itself called upon, to write only the truth. That is what this trial is about today.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I see no guilt in myself whatsoever. In the very first days after my detention, law enforcement officers told me: &#8216;Alexander Borisovich, your fate will not be decided in court, but here and now.&#8217; They also lied to me, claiming that my wife had been hospitalised with a heart attack. &#8216;Admit your guilt and everything will be fine.&#8217;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But what exactly am I guilty of? My grandfather was executed for Belarus, and you think I will be afraid of your four-year sentence, Mr Public Prosecutor? Look me in the eye. Never.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I feel sorry only for my loved ones: my sick wife and my grandson Donat, whom I had hoped to teach new words. Now his mother has to find the words to explain to a child why his grandfather is in prison–a place for real criminals, and criminals alone. I believe she will find the right words, and my conscience will remain clear. My grandson will never have to be ashamed of his grandfather&#8217;s name. I regret the projects I will clearly never be able to bring to fruition, as four years will not be enough, and I do not believe in that other life. I regret what has happened to &#8216;Regіянальная газета&#8217;, my employees and its readers, who have been deprived of the sincere friend that &#8216;RG&#8217; was. The events surrounding &#8216;Regіянальная газета&#8217; after the summer of 2021 destroyed a magnificent project that worked for Belarus and in the name of Belarus. I do not admit my guilt, and I never will.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Once again, thank you all for supporting me. My endless gratitude goes to my family, friends and colleagues, to everyone who attended the hearings over these seven days, to those who could not attend but were thinking of me, to those who wrote me letters in Zhodzina, sent me parcels and did so much more. Thank you all. Thank you, Grigory Fyodorovich, for our work together. Thank you, Honourable Court, for giving me the opportunity to deliver my final statement.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Thank you to all the other parties to these proceedings, and to everyone present. I ask the Honourable Court to add the written version of my statement to the case file, because what is written remains.</p>
